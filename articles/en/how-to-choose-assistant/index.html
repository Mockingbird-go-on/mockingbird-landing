How to Choose an AI Interview Assistant: 6 Criteria

Guide2026-09-24·6 min

In short: a good AI interview assistant runs locally, recognizes speech in real time, answers within 2–5 seconds and never sends your data to the cloud. If a tool requires constant internet or uploads audio to a server, that's a risk both for privacy and stability.

1. Speech processing: local or cloud

This is the main criterion. Cloud services send your voice to someone else's servers — latency, internet dependency, potential leaks. Local recognition runs on your computer. Details — “Offline or cloud”.

2. Accuracy and language support

  • Is your language properly supported.
  • How the model handles technical terms.
  • Whether there's phonetic term correction.
  • Whether you can change the model size.

3. Answer latency

The comfortable threshold is 2–5 seconds from the end of the question. Longer than that and the conversation loses pace. Model analysis — “Why large-v3-turbo”.

4. Privacy and discretion

Must-haves: Invisible Mode, one-action mute, no intrusive telemetry. How it works — “Invisible Mode”.

5. Platform and easy installation

You need builds for Windows and Linux, a simple installer or a single file, clear updates and a changelog.

6. Cost and openness

Open source means transparency: you can see what goes to the network. Free without subscriptions removes the risk of losing access at the wrong moment.

FAQ

What matters most when choosing?

Local speech processing. It determines privacy, latency and internet independence all at once.

Is it worth paying for an assistant?

There are free open source solutions that fully cover the tasks. Paying only makes sense for unique features.

Are built-in browser AI features enough?

For interviews — no: they don't recognize speech in real time and don't mix in résumé context.
